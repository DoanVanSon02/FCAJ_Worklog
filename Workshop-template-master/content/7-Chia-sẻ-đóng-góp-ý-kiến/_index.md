### Reflections and Overall Evaluation

**1. Working Environment**  
During my internship, I found the working environment quite friendly and open, encouraging interns to take the initiative in learning. I could focus on my assigned tasks while also having room to independently explore AWS concepts that I had never encountered before. What I appreciated was that the environment did not create excessive pressure but still required everyone to demonstrate self-discipline and responsibility for their work.

**2. Support from Mentors and the Team Admin**  
During the program, I encountered quite a few Cloud-related issues as well as some other matters, and the mentors and team admins always offered enthusiastic support. In particular, the Cloud knowledge they shared during workshop sessions and while I was developing my own workshop helped me understand that it is not enough to simply make a system work; I also need to explain why particular services were chosen and how those services connect with one another. Through this, I gradually improved my approach to thinking through and presenting a technical solution.

**3. Relevance of the Work to My Major**  
I am majoring in **Computer Networks and Communications**, so before joining the program, my knowledge mainly covered computer networks, network administration, and some system fundamentals. When I began learning about AWS, I noticed that Cloud includes many topics related to what I had studied, such as servers, storage, access permissions, networking, and system monitoring, but the practical implementation introduced many new aspects.

While working on the project **“Building a Backup & Recovery System for a Web Server on AWS”**, I had the opportunity to combine my knowledge of servers and networking with Cloud services such as EC2, EBS, S3, IAM, AWS Backup, and CloudWatch. This helped me better understand how the knowledge I had learned at university could be applied in a Cloud environment.

**4. Opportunities for Learning and Personal Development**  
The internship helped me gain knowledge in many areas that I had not previously explored in depth, particularly Cloud services. While working on the project, I not only followed instructions but also had to research documentation, investigate errors, and try different configurations before achieving the desired results.

Through this process, I improved my ability to learn independently, find documentation, analyze problems, and verify the results after each implementation step. I also realized that I need to understand the fundamentals of each service rather than simply memorize the steps performed in the AWS Console.

**5. Corporate Culture and Team Spirit**  
I felt that the working environment valued initiative and independent learning. Each intern needed to take responsibility for their own progress, but could still discuss difficulties with others and receive support.

This helped me gradually develop the habit of investigating problems on my own first, then gathering the unresolved points to discuss with others. In addition, learning and exchanging ideas with other interns gave me more perspectives on solving problems and motivated me throughout the process of completing the Workshop.

**6. Policies and Benefits for Interns**  
During the internship, I was given opportunities to learn new concepts while gaining hands-on experience with AWS. I appreciated that the program not only required project completion but also included knowledge-sharing sessions, guidance, and reviews to help interns better understand real-world working practices.

Learning about Cloud, gaining hands-on experience with AWS services, and attending knowledge-sharing sessions while working on the project were valuable experiences for me. Through this internship, I gained a clearer direction for continuing to study AWS, Cloud Computing, and topics related to my major in Computer Networks and Communications.

As someone who especially enjoys coffee, I really liked the coffee station at the office, which helped me feel energized and stay alert while studying.
